### Q2. Connect houses to electricity with minimum cable

There are N houses in a single straight line along a street. You are given an array of their positions/coordinates (e.g., House A at mile 1, House B at mile 5, House C at mile 6). Some houses already have electricity ON (1), others are OFF (0). At least one house already has power.

You need to run cables along the street so every dark house gets electricity.
- You can connect any house to its immediate neighbour.
- The cost of a cable is the distance between the two houses connected (e.g., connecting mile 1 to mile 5 costs 5 - 1 = 4).
- Once a dark house is connected to a powered house, it lights up and can pass power on to the next house.

What is the shortest total length of cable needed so that every house has electricity?

Example:
```
House locations: 1, 5, 6
Power status: [OFF, ON, ON]
Output: 5
```
Connect house at 1 to house at 5 (cost 4). The houses at 5 and 6 are both ON but still need to be part of the connected network, so connect them (cost 1). Total = 4 + 1 = 5.

### Q2. Largest Odd-Sized Palindromic Square Submatrix

You are given an N x M integer grid. Find the largest odd-sized square submatrix (1x1, 3x3, 5x5, ...) such that opposite elements are equal when the square is rotated by 180 degrees.  
For example, this 3x3 matrix is valid:  
1 2 3  
4 5 4  
3 2 1  
Here, the first row is the reverse of the last row, and the middle row is itself a palindrome.  
Similarly, for a 5x5 square, the 1st row should be the reverse of the 5th row, the 2nd row should be the reverse of the 4th row, and the middle row should be a palindrome.  
Return the maximum number of elements in such a square (area, not side length).  
Example:  
Input:  
1 2 3  
4 5 4  
3 2 1  
Output: 9  
If no valid square larger than 1x1 exists, return 1.

Given an m x n grid of free cells (0) and obstacles (1), you start at the top-left cell (0,0) and need to reach the bottom-right cell (m-1,n-1). You cannot move through or onto obstacle cells. Determine whether a valid path exists from the top-left cell to the bottom-right cell. You may move between adjacent cells in the allowed directions (e.g., up, down, left, right).  
Example grid:  
0 0 0  
1 1 0  
0 0 0  
A valid path exists: (0,0) -> (0,1) -> (0,2) -> (1,2) -> (2,2), so the answer is YES.
